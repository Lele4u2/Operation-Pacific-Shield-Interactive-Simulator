# 🎮 OPS GAME EXPERIENCE — INTERACTIVE LEARNING, HINTS & SMART ASSISTANCE

## 🧠 Learn OPS Your Way

Operation Pacific Shield should be fun, immersive, challenging, and easy to learn.

Every player learns differently. Some people want detailed explanations, while others understand better through short instructions, visuals, audio, or hands-on practice.

OPS will include an interactive learning system that allows players to choose how they want to learn.

**You should never feel lost, confused, or stuck without knowing what to do next.**

## 🎯 Choose Your Experience

Before starting a mission, players can select their preferred learning style.

### 🟢 Beginner — Teach Me Everything

Designed for people who have never played OPS.

- Explain every position in simple English.
- Highlight which game pieces to select.
- Demonstrate how to complete objectives.
- Explain military abbreviations.
- Provide step-by-step instructions.
- Pause to explain important decisions.
- Show why an action is correct or incorrect.
- Offer unlimited practice and retries.

### 🔵 Guided — Give Me Hints

For players who understand the basics but still want assistance.

- Provide optional hints.
- Highlight missing requirements.
- Explain why a particular action is unavailable.
- Identify which organization can provide support.
- Give reminders when an objective is incomplete.
- Allow players to request an explanation without automatically completing the task.

### 🟠 Independent — Let Me Figure It Out

For players who want to practice with minimal assistance.

- No automatic hints.
- Standard mission instructions.
- Normal role responsibilities.
- Decisions and consequences.
- Optional help when requested.
- Detailed After-Action Review.

### 🔴 Challenge — Test My Knowledge

For experienced players who want a demanding fictional exercise.

- Limited hints during gameplay.
- Competing priorities.
- Unexpected scenario events.
- Multiple pending coordination requirements.
- Role-specific decision-making.
- More detailed performance feedback at mission completion.

Difficulty must not change source-defined rules in an authorized training mode. Any additional fictional challenges should be identified as custom practice features.

## 💡 The Smart Hint Button

Every interactive workstation must have a visible **HINT** button.

When selected, the player can choose:

**Hint 1 — Small Clue**

Tell me what general problem I should be looking for without giving the answer.

**Hint 2 — Point Me in the Right Direction**

Show me which board, resource, or organization may help.

**Hint 3 — Walk Me Through It**

Explain the next required interaction using simple language and highlight the relevant game controls.

**Explain Like I'm New**

Use everyday language, short sentences, and examples.

**Show Me**

Demonstrate the interaction in a practice environment without automatically completing my live mission.

**Read It to Me**

Read the instructions aloud with adjustable narration speed and subtitles.

Hints must use the actual current game state. They should never recommend an unavailable resource or action prohibited for the player's position.

## 🗣️ Interactive OPS Training Advisor

Create an optional on-screen animated training advisor.

The advisor should respond to questions such as:

- "What am I supposed to do?"
- "What is an FOS?"
- "Why can't I move this counter?"
- "What does this objective mean?"
- "Who do I need to contact?"
- "What am I missing?"
- "Explain this in easier words."
- "What happens if I do this?"
- "Where do I click next?"

The advisor should offer short answers first, with an option for more detail.

It must distinguish between verified information, available game options, and fictional scenario assumptions.

The advisor cannot secretly complete objectives, make unauthorized choices, or reveal hidden Game Master information.

---

## 🗺️ Interactive Visual Guidance

When a beginner opens a board, show a short optional tutorial.

Example:

**STEP 1:** Click the highlighted fictional Forward Operating Site.

**STEP 2:** Open the site's available objectives.

**STEP 3:** Select the assessment task.

**STEP 4:** Review the information and identify missing requirements.

**STEP 5:** Choose an authorized next action.

The game should visually highlight the relevant button, hex, board section, or counter.

When the player completes a step, move the tutorial to the next step.

If the player selects the wrong control, explain what it does instead of simply showing an error.

---

## 🎲 Make Learning Entertaining

Add optional training activities that practice the actual game controls:

### Resource Challenge

Give the player fictional resources and ask them to complete a support objective using valid allocations.

### Aircraft Loading Practice

Let players drag simulated cargo counters onto aircraft loading boards and receive immediate feedback on the game's fictional loading constraints.

### Find the Missing Requirement

Show an incomplete objective and challenge the player to identify why it cannot be completed.

### Mission Commander Challenge

Give players several fictional station reports and ask them to coordinate the next authorized action.

### RFI Practice

Let players practice selecting requests, reviewing results, and deciding what information is still needed.

### Team Coordination Challenge

Two or more players must communicate and complete an objective together.

AI teammates fill any vacant positions.

These are optional learning activities and should not replace the main OPS simulation.

---

## 🎧 Audio Learning and Accessibility

Include:

- Spoken mission briefings.
- Optional narrated tutorials.
- Text-to-speech for explanations.
- Adjustable speaking speed.
- Subtitles.
- Mute controls.
- Keyboard alternatives.
- Accessible colors and symbols.
- Adjustable text size.
- A simple-English glossary.

Every important instruction must remain available in text.

---

## 🏆 Reward Learning Without Changing OPS Scoring

Add a separate optional training progression system.

Players may earn achievements for:

- Completing their first tutorial.
- Correctly identifying an objective requirement.
- Successfully coordinating a support request.
- Finishing a practice mission.
- Completing a mission without hints.
- Improving performance in a previously difficult area.

These rewards must not change official OPS Mission Points or override scenario outcomes.

---

## 👥 Helpful Learning in Solo and Multiplayer

In solo play, computer-controlled teammates should remain functional while the player requests help.

In team play, each participant can choose their own learning assistance level.

One beginner might use detailed hints while another player works independently.

An instructor can choose whether hints are permitted during a graded or supervised exercise.

Hints must never reveal hidden information from another station or the Game Master.

---

## ✅ Definition of a Working Learning System

The training system is operational only when a beginner can:

1. Enter the game without knowing OPS.
2. Select a position.
3. Learn the position's responsibilities.
4. Open an interactive board.
5. Choose a real gameplay objective.
6. Request a hint.
7. Receive guidance appropriate to the current situation.
8. Perform the action themselves.
9. Understand what changed.
10. Complete a playable practice mission.
11. Review what they learned.

**The player should become more capable with every mission, not simply more familiar with clicking buttons.**

### FINAL DESIGN GOAL

Make OPS feel like a polished strategy video game with the flexibility of a personal instructor.

Players should be able to learn visually, listen to explanations, request clues, experiment safely, work with AI teammates, or play with real people.

**Make the game challenging when players want a challenge, and easy to understand when they need help.**
